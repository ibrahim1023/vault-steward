# Customer Interviews

Between June 10 and July 8, the product team spoke with twelve administrators
from agencies, internal operations groups, and small professional-services
teams. Each participant had purchased a workspace but had not yet rolled out a
shared workflow to their wider team.

## What administrators told us

Most participants did not describe setup as difficult in isolation. They
described it as risky: nobody wanted to select a permission model and then
discover a month later that a contractor could see the wrong information. The
common request was a confirmation that the first configuration was sensible,
not more fields in the setup form.

One agency operations lead said that a short explanation of what each role can
do would remove most of their hesitation. A finance manager asked for a way to
prove that no client data was imported during the trial. Both themes support a
small validation report and careful explanatory copy.

## Implications for the first release

The wizard should use progressive disclosure. It should lead with a recommended
template, show the reasoning in ordinary language, and make changes reversible.
The validation report should distinguish warnings from blocking errors. It
should not attempt to score the organization or claim that a configuration is
objectively correct.
